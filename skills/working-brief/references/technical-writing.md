# Technical-writing influences

This editorial workflow uses independently phrased, selected principles from these sources, not the full guides or their repository-specific automation:

- [Jstack technical-writing reference](https://github.com/gutierrezje/jstack/blob/ae5b5b569010e905b4c8ff49c9e26516608117ad/skills/jesus-mode/references/technical-writing.md).
- [Upstream pstack technical-writing skill](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/blob/4dd1d05818d19792e7cd6e818f1667f95f7e1dbe/pstack/skills/technical-writing/SKILL.md).
- [Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/): separate the reader's need to do something from their need to understand or look up information.
- [Google developer documentation style](https://developers.google.com/style): clarify the actor and action, use meaningful headings, and place conditions before the relevant instruction.

Jstack/pstack emphasize everyday words, consistent names, unambiguous syntax, natural rhythm, and writing that remains understandable to a tired reader. Apply those ideas to the brief's editing pass. Keep the original evidence and qualifications; clarity is not permission to turn uncertain statements into facts.

Use the distinctions flexibly: a working brief may have a decision-oriented opening and a clearly separated reference appendix. Do not force the whole artifact into one rigid documentation category, or strip necessary context to meet sentence-length targets. Do not modify jstack, pstack, or another writing skill as a side effect.

The task-state and authorization distinctions in Working brief are additional safeguards for personal-assistant deliverables. They are not claims that the cited technical-writing guides provide a task-management system.
