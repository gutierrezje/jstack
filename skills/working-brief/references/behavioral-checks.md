# Behavioral checks

When revising the skill, give an independent evaluator one fixture below and the
skill, without prescribing an answer. Ask for a short working brief, not a PDF;
rendering checks belong to the selected artifact workflow. Evaluate the result
against the invariants after the evaluator finishes. These are editorial regression
fixtures, not evidence of user-tested readability or universal quality scores.

## Mixed evidence and approval

**Request:** Make this project note easy to act on for the founder on a phone.

**Source packet:** On May 2 the founder said, “Use one search across places, drinks,
and people.” Issue 18 proposes following and a friends feed. The May 3 code audit
found public profiles and people search, but no follow table in the inspected
schema. The note calls issue 17 completed, without a link. An owner-menu pilot is
proposed but not approved; neither its size nor success criteria is settled.
Anonymous people search and one-way versus reciprocal relationships remain open.
The draft tests require reciprocal relationships. The recommendation introduces
a read-only preview for external places, absent from its implementation sequence.
There is no decision on what drink search contains if the pilot is rejected.

**Check after generation:**
- The search direction stays confirmed and attributed to the founder.
- Profiles/search are observed; the feed is planned, not implemented.
- Issue 17's completion remains unverified.
- Both open policy choices stay open; tests do not silently settle reciprocity.
- Options have consequences; any proposed numbers are labeled assumptions or
  recommendations, not agreed targets.
- A proposed pilot-independent scope is clearly proposed, not invented agreement.
- The new preview appears in the proposed sequence or explicitly outside scope.
- No recommendation becomes authorized implementation.

## Consistency under compression

**Request:** Shorten this update without losing the decision or evidence.

**Source packet:** A proposed partner rollout depends on approval of moderation
staffing. The sponsor has not answered. The opening calls the rollout “confirmed”;
the appendix says “proposed.” A public launch is described as done, but the only
receipt is a passing test run. Page one moves privacy policy to “can wait,” while
acceptance tests require that policy. Detail tables are wider than a phone and
place authorization limits in tiny footnotes.

**Check after generation:** The brief preserves the unanswered decision and the
actual test result, aligns the summary with details and tests, supplies a labeled
fallback recommendation or names the missing scope decision, and does not claim
public delivery. It improves or flags the detail layout instead of declaring the
whole document phone-readable from its new opening alone.
